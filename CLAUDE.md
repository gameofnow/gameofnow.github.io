## What The Game Øf Now Is

A practice — 20 years in development — of transforming any moment into conscious, courageous, creative play. The three C's.

It is a question that has an infinite number of possible responses, depending on the qualities of your awareness, attention, imagination, courage, capacity to communicate, willpower, energy, adaptability, and more.

The core question, asked at the threshold before entering a field of play:
**"What is The Game Øf Now?"**

Not "what is the challenge?" (too narrow, performance-oriented).
Not "what is the game?" (too vague).
The word *Now* anchors attention to this specific moment. The question opens the field rather than closing it.

The game can be played:

**Solo:** self-observation, experimentation, following impulses.

**With another (2 players):** shared field, alternating moves, listening as part of the game.

**In groups:** three or more. Collective, emergent, facilitated.

---

**Open field:** you step through without knowing what will happen. No pre-set objective.

**Closed field:** you have a specific task or context (washing, a document to write, a conversation to have), which includes elements. For example, in washing: the clothes, the basket, the walk from the washing machine to the clothes horse. Each action can have a different adverb — a different quality. You can also add actions, like singing. You can add objects with qualities (nouns and adjectives). You then weave these elements into your game. You can reject them or add new ones at any time, in any flow.

These are not two different games. One game, two entry points.

---

**Timings:** You can play for any length of time, from one second to eternity (karmically to Earth — but who knows where else). Set a timer for the duration and a warning timer perhaps five or ten minutes before the end, so you can prepare for the ending.

## The Philosophy (Key Ideas)

**On energy:** Conscious thinking and imagining are assumed to require high energy — this is why people default to autopilot. A lot of energy is also wasted on overthinking, particularly worrying. But *flowing* attention — following images and impulses without the editor — requires *less* energy. The editor, and the effort to adapt to social conditioning, is what drains people.

**On improv:** People will compare TGØN to improv theatre. The distinction: improv is a performance context. TGØN is a life practice — in the kitchen, in the car, alone, anywhere. "Improv is improv. The Game of Now is the Game of Now."

**On state:** The state you are in when you play makes all the difference. Many people live moment to moment in autopilot, anxiety, or stress. Some are very peaceful but don't do much. When you consciously enter the playing field of The Game Øf Now, you can choose which state to enter. You can adjust to be more peaceful or more energised. Some of the most alive decisions come from awareness of discomfort, urgency, even fear. The question is not "am I at peace?" but "am I fully present to what this moment actually requires?"

**On playing vs training:** Playing the game and training the capacities are different activities that support each other. The six core capacities: Awareness, Attention, Curiosity, Imagination, Courage, Will. We have many more. *Note: always include Curiosity — it may be the driving force behind evolution.*

**On autopilot:** The game begins when you notice you have been on autopilot, or when you remember The Game Øf Now. Ideally, play immediately — even if for one minute. The Game as a spirit, a being — it has nourished Graham for 20 years. He relates to it as something alive. So can you. It's a choice.

**On experimentation:** In the past, religious leaders set the rules of the game and people obeyed through fear. Tyrants, despots, and dictators did the same — their rules, play or be punished. Now scientists set the rules of what is possible, but that is also just a game. You can choose to imagine and experiment. It's your game. One meta-rule worth holding: don't do anything in any game that you would not like to experience yourself. (Not so easy.)

**On sovereignty:** Playing from your centre is very different from playing when pulled off-centre by others. Charismatic people, highly enthusiastic people, can pull you from your centre. So can good salespeople — using excitement or fear. "Hurry while stocks last." If you are employed, you are playing someone else's game. Even self-employment can mean playing the market's game rather than your own. When we can explore and experiment in freedom, we can feel truly alive.

**On trust:** Trust is built through time, through showing up honestly, and through the principle that you can leave at any time — or observe first, from the audience. Testimonials will help. It is a design challenge.
